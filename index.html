My challenge.

One real thing you are trying to move that you cannot simply direct. The same challenge travels through all five stops, so pick something live.

BEFORE YOU START
The order matters: skip a stop and the influence usually breaks there. Most conversations break at Hear (we stop listening) or at Take it forward (we never quite ask). Notice which one is your edge as you go.
S

See the field.

Map who actually decides the fate of your goal, and why. Attention is the scarcest thing a leader has; spend it on the right people.

How to map: for each stakeholder, set Power (their ability to affect the outcome) and Interest (how much they care). The tool places them on the grid and names the move. For the political ones, add the salience read: Legitimacy (is their claim valid?) and Urgency (is their need time-critical?). High power plus both flags makes them definitive: they demand attention now. Finish by naming the RACI and the conversation each person needs.

WATCH OUTS · SEE
  • Do not confuse loud with powerful. The person making the most noise is not always the one who decides.
  • Maps are living, not fixed. A quiet, legitimate stakeholder becomes a priority the instant urgency spikes. Re-map at each phase.
  • Check for the high-power, low-interest person you have been treating as "keep informed". That is where timelines quietly slip.
H

Hear what they value.

Diagnose the currency each of your three trades in before you pitch anything. Listen more than you speak.

The five currencies (Cohen & Bradford): Inspiration (vision, meaning, doing what matters) · Task (resources, help, faster ways to get the job done) · Position (recognition, reputation, visibility) · Relationship (belonging, being included, personal support) · Personal (gratitude, ownership, comfort).
Listen with RASA (Treasure): ReceiveAppreciateSummariseAsk
WATCH OUTS · HEAR
  • A complaint is a value showing itself. When they push back, they are telling you what they care about. Write it down.
  • Diagnose before you pitch. If you cannot name their currency, you are not ready to make the case.
I

Idea worth backing.

One Big Idea, story and data, in their currency. Lead with why.

WATCH OUTS · IDEA
  • One idea, not five. Every extra ask weakens the main one.
  • Beware the curse of knowledge: you have lived this problem, they have not. Tell the story before the spreadsheet.
  • Put the number in their currency, not yours.
F

Flex and deliver.

Meet their style, not yours. And keep your feet when it gets bumpy.

Open in their thinking preference (HBDI): Analytical wants the facts and the numbers first · Practical wants the plan, steps and risks · Relational wants who is affected and how people feel · Experimental wants the big picture and what is possible.
Under pressure: structure your answer as WhatSo whatNow what and hold PaceSpaceGrace: slow down, let silence work, stay generous.
WATCH OUTS · FLEX
  • Your default opening is your preference, not theirs. Script the first 30 seconds in their style.
  • Pushback is information, not attack. Pace, space, grace, then What / So what / Now what.
T

Take it forward.

Influence often fails at the last metre: a great case with no actual ask. Make yours, and make it easy to say yes to.

The anatomy of a low-friction ask: Specific (one action, not a program of works) · Bounded (a window, not forever) · Reciprocal (what you will carry in return) · Dated (a first move with a date on it).
When you hit a wall, the magic question: "What would it take?" It moves the exchange from confrontation to collaboration and often unearths the real obstacle.

Build my ask.

Land the agreement with SBI+A.

The tool you already know from Leading Teams, now closing an influence conversation.

WATCH OUTS · TAKE IT FORWARD
  • The ask you are nervous to make is far more likely to get a yes than you think (Bohns). The most common reason we do not get what we need is that we never actually asked.
  • No date, no deal. An agreement without a first move and a date is a warm conversation, not influence.
  • Warm the ground first: give before you ask, and speak as "we", to a shared win.
